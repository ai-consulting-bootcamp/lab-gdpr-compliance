# Part 1 - Audit worksheet

## Phase 1 - Fact pattern

StyleNest GmbH is a mid-size online fashion retailer based in Hamburg, with about 250 employees and around 1.2 million customers in Germany, Austria and the Netherlands. It wants to launch an AI recommendation engine that shows each visitor personalised products on the homepage, product pages and in marketing emails. The engine would use:
- first-party cookie data (pages viewed, clicks, time on page, device type)
- purchase history and basket contents
- account data (name, email, address, age band, and the gender selected at sign-up)
- email engagement (opens and clicks)

The data would be sent to **InsightLoop Inc.**, a **US-based analytics vendor** that hosts the data in the US, trains the recommendation model, and also offers "lookalike audiences" for advertising. Some purchases (e.g. maternity wear, adaptive clothing, plus-size ranges) could reveal sensitive information about customers. Recommendations also decide which products and **personalised discounts** each customer sees. No human reviews individual recommendations.

*Assumption (confirmed after peer review): this describes the **proposed** setup. InsightLoop's model training and lookalike features are offered in its standard contract, but are **not yet active** for StyleNest. The audit assesses whether they may be switched on.*

## CFU 1 - Recognize

- **Personal data categories:**
  - identifiers: name, email, postal address, customer ID, cookie ID, IP address, device ID
  - demographics: age band, gender
  - behavioural data: browsing, clicks, time on page
  - transaction data: orders, basket, returns
  - email engagement
  - **inferred data**: interest segments and predicted preferences
- **Special-category data (Article 9):** none is collected directly, but it **can be inferred**:
  - pregnancy (maternity wear) and disability (adaptive clothing) count as health data
  - collections such as modest fashion or Pride ranges could reveal religion or sexual orientation

  CJEU case law (e.g. C-184/20 and C-252/21) treats data that *indirectly reveals* sensitive information as falling under Article 9.
- **Does data cross an EU border?** **Yes.** Data flows from the EU to **InsightLoop in the US**, and possibly to US ad platforms for lookalike audiences.

## Section B - Risk and rights

**Special-category data (Article 9)?**
Yes, it is **inferable**. Purchases of maternity or adaptive clothing can reveal health data, and some ranges could reveal religion or sexual orientation. Article 9 allows no legitimate-interests basis, so StyleNest must either **exclude these product categories from profiling** (recommended) or obtain **explicit consent** (Art. 9(2)(a)).

**Automated decision-making (Article 22)?**
**Not for product recommendations**, because seeing different products has no legal or similarly significant effect. Personalised discounts are **borderline**: if some customers systematically get much worse prices, that could be a "similarly significant effect". **Safeguard:** discounts only ever add a benefit (no higher prices), differences are capped, the rules are set by humans, and the customer is told that the price is personalised.

**Is a DPIA required?** **Yes.** At least five of the EDPB's nine criteria apply, and two are enough for a DPIA to be generally required.

| # | EDPB criterion | Applies? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evaluation or scoring, including profiling | ✅ Yes, customer profiles and predictions |
| 2 | Automated decision with legal or similar effect | ⚠️ Borderline (discounts) |
| 3 | Systematic monitoring | ✅ Yes, tracking of browsing behaviour |
| 4 | Sensitive data | ✅ Yes, inferable health and other Article 9 data |
| 5 | Large scale | ✅ Yes, about 1.2 million customers |
| 6 | Matching or combining datasets | ✅ Yes, cookies + purchases + account + email data |
| 7 | Vulnerable data subjects | ⚠️ Possibly, if under-16s shop (check the age bands) |
| 8 | Innovative technology | ✅ Yes, machine-learning recommendation model |
| 9 | Prevents people from exercising a right or using a service | ❌ No |

**Likely data subject friction points**
1. **Right to object to profiling and direct marketing (Art. 21).** For marketing this right is absolute, so it must work instantly in every channel.
2. **Right of access (Art. 15).** Customers may ask "why do you think I'm pregnant?", so StyleNest must be able to show inferred segments too.
3. **Right to erasure (Art. 17).** Data must be deleted at InsightLoop as well, and removing someone's influence from a trained model is hard.

Withdrawing cookie consent must also stop tracking straight away.

**Controller / processor split**

| Entity | Role |
|---|---|
| StyleNest GmbH | **Controller.** It decides the purposes and means. |
| InsightLoop Inc. | **Processor** for hosting and training StyleNest's model on instructions. It becomes a **controller / joint controller** if it reuses data for its own models or for lookalike audiences. |
| Email service provider | **Processor** |
| Ad platforms (lookalikes, pixels) | **Joint controllers** with StyleNest for collecting and sharing the data (*Fashion ID*) |

**DPA needed?**
- **Yes, an Article 28 DPA with InsightLoop**, the email service provider, and through InsightLoop all its sub-processors.
- An **Article 26 joint controller arrangement** with ad platforms, if lookalikes are ever used.

## Section C - Law stacking

| Law | Check |
|---|---|
| **AI Act** | A product recommender for online retail is **minimal risk**: it is not in Annex III and not an Article 5 practice, as long as it doesn't exploit vulnerabilities (Art. 5(1)(b)), e.g. targeting financially stressed customers with "buy now" pressure. The AI Act adds little beyond **AI literacy (Art. 4)**. GDPR does the heavy lifting here. *(Side note: if StyleNest ever becomes a marketplace platform, the Digital Services Act's recommender transparency rules would also apply.)* |
| **ePrivacy** | **Yes.** Cookies, pixels and email open tracking need **consent** under Art. 5(3) ePrivacy (Germany: §25 TDDDG). This **overrides GDPR's flexibility**: StyleNest cannot rely on legitimate interests for cookie-based tracking, only for the later use of data it already holds lawfully. |
| **Data Act** | **Mostly not applicable.** There are no connected products or IoT data. The only relevant part is the **cloud switching rules**, which could help StyleNest move its data and model away from InsightLoop if needed. |

# Part 2 - Client recommendation memo

**To:** Chief Technology Officer and Legal Team, StyleNest GmbH
**From:** Barsha Mishra, Data Protection & AI Consultant
**Date:** 17 November 2026
**Re:** GDPR review - AI recommendation engine with InsightLoop

**Bottom line: Go with conditions.** The recommendation engine can be built lawfully, but not as currently designed: tracking without consent, sensitive purchase data feeding the profiles, and an unrestricted US vendor would each create real GDPR exposure.

**Top three actions, in this order**

1. **Lock down InsightLoop before any live customer data is sent, including for testing or pilots.** Sign an Article 28 data processing agreement that limits InsightLoop to processing on your instructions only. It may not train its own models and may not offer lookalike audiences on your data. Confirm InsightLoop's EU-US Data Privacy Framework certification. If it isn't certified, sign Standard Contractual Clauses and complete a transfer impact assessment. Get a full list of its sub-processors.
2. **Run a DPIA before launch.** The project meets at least five of the EDPB's high-risk criteria. Make one design decision in the DPIA: **exclude maternity, adaptive and similar product categories from profiling**, so the model cannot infer health or other sensitive traits. Keep personalised discounts to "extra savings only", never higher prices.
3. **Fix consent and transparency.** Add a "personalisation" option to your cookie banner, and only use browsing data from visitors who opt in. Update the privacy notice to explain profiling, InsightLoop and the US transfer. Add a one-click "turn off personalisation" setting in the customer account and in every marketing email. Tell customers when a discount is personalised.

**Residual risks, even if you do all of the above**

- **Hidden inferences.** Even with sensitive categories excluded, the model may still pick up sensitive patterns indirectly, for example through colour, size or brand choices. Test outputs regularly.
- **US transfer risk.** The EU-US Data Privacy Framework has been legally challenged. If it falls, you must switch to contractual safeguards quickly, so keep them ready.
- **Erasure from the model.** Deleting a customer's raw data is easy, but removing their influence from a trained model is not. Plan regular retraining on cleaned data.

The lower consent rates you can expect will also reduce personalisation coverage. That is a business cost, not a compliance gap.

*This memo is a consulting assessment, not legal advice. Lawful-basis choices marked "TBD" need confirmation by your data protection officer or external counsel.*

**2. Legitimate interests assessment - purchase-history profiling (summary)**
- **Purpose:** show existing customers relevant products, a recognised commercial interest (Recital 47).
- **Necessity:** it uses only data StyleNest already holds from orders. There is no device tracking and no third-party data, and less intrusive options (e.g. "bestsellers" for all) do not achieve the purpose.
- **Balancing:** customers reasonably expect shop recommendations based on their own purchases. Their interests are protected by:
  - excluding sensitive product categories (maternity, adaptive clothing)
  - a one-click opt-out in the account
  - a 24-month retention limit
  - no data sharing for advertising

  **Result: balancing favours StyleNest**, subject to these safeguards. The LIA will be documented in the DPIA.

**3. Confirmation:** no live customer data, including for testing or pilots, will be sent to InsightLoop until (a) the Article 28 DPA with the no-own-training clause is signed, (b) the DPF certification is verified or SCCs and a transfer impact assessment are in place, and (c) the consent banner, privacy notice and opt-out are live. ## Debrief (comparing our audits, 17 November 2026)

**Where we agreed**
- **A DPIA is required.** We both identified large-scale profiling, combining datasets, systematic monitoring and possible sensitive inferences.
- **The AI Act adds little.** The recommender is minimal risk, and GDPR and ePrivacy do the real work.
- **Cookies need ePrivacy consent**, and InsightLoop must be tightly restricted before any data flows.

**Where we diverged, and which reading is stronger**
1. **Legitimate interests for purchase-history profiling.** My first draft relied on legitimate interests without a written balancing test. My partner argued it is only defensible with a documented LIA. **Her reading is stronger.** Legitimate interests is an accountability-based basis, so without a written assessment it cannot be shown to the regulator. I added the LIA.
2. **Existing-customer email exception.** I applied §7(3) UWG broadly. My partner pointed out its conditions are narrow. **Her reading is stronger.** I now list all four conditions and require consent for everyone else.
3. **Cookie consent vs GDPR basis.** I had treated the cookie consent as also covering the profiling. We agreed it is **two legal steps** (ePrivacy for device access, then GDPR Art. 6 for processing). Consent remains the right GDPR basis here, but it has to be shown separately.
4. **"Implemented" vs "possible" processing.** My partner noted that the fact pattern did not establish InsightLoop's training and lookalike features as active. We agreed to treat them as **proposed features to block contractually**, not existing breaches. I added this assumption to the fact pattern.

**Article 22:** we agreed that ordinary recommendations do not meet the "similarly significant effects" threshold, and that personalised discounts need a separate check. This was already in Section B.

**Partner sign-off (17 November 2026):** my partner confirmed that the revisions address her points (documented LIA, §7(3) UWG conditions, separating cookie consent from the GDPR lawful basis), and agreed to this summary as our **joint debrief conclusion**.

# Reinforce

**DPIA outline - customer profiling for recommendations**
1. **Description of processing:** data sources, the InsightLoop flow, model training, and the outputs (recommendations, discounts).
2. **Necessity and proportionality:** why each data field is needed, removing gender if it isn't predictive, and excluding sensitive categories.
3. **Risks to individuals:** sensitive inferences, unfair price differences, US government access, loss of control over profiles.
4. **Mitigations:** exclusion lists, consent gating, discount caps, pseudonymisation before transfer, retention limits, regular output testing.

**One DPA clause I would insist on**
*"The Processor shall not use Customer Personal Data, or any model, embedding or derived data created from it, to train, improve or benchmark models or services for itself or any third party, and shall delete or return all such data and derived models on termination."* This stops InsightLoop from quietly becoming a controller of StyleNest's customer data.

**If StyleNest also serves UK customers**
The bottom line stays the same. Under UK GDPR, transfers to the US need the **UK Extension to the DPF** (if InsightLoop is certified for it) or the UK **IDTA / Addendum to the SCCs**. UK cookie rules (PECR) also require consent, and the ICO would be the regulator for those customers.
