# Case B fact pattern: contact-centre "wellbeing assistant"

**For:** Friedrich, common ground for the GDPR lab · **From:** Nicolò · 17 Nov 2026

Based on your Case B brief. Anything your brief didn't specify (geography, vendor, call volume, end-clients) is marked *assumed*. Tell me if you'd set any of it differently, so we audit the same facts.

---

The client is a **contact-centre outsourcer with about 600 agents** at two sites in Germany (*assumed*). It runs inbound customer-service lines for **telecom and insurance companies** (*assumed*), handling roughly **30,000 calls a day** (*assumed*), with callers mostly in Germany and Austria (EU).

To tackle high sick leave and turnover, it wants a **"wellbeing assistant"** bought from a **US-headquartered emotion-AI SaaS vendor**. The vendor processes audio and video in its US cloud region (*assumed*).

The tool does three things:
- analyses each **agent's voice tone during calls** and **webcam images at the workstation** to detect stress and frustration in real time;
- shows **team leads a per-agent dashboard**, and feeds these signals into **shift planning and coaching conversations**;
- scores **each customer's mood** from their voice on every call.

Personal data involved:
- **Agents:** name and employee ID, voice, face images, inferred emotional and stress states, shift and coaching records.
- **Callers:** phone number, voice, call content (which for insurance lines can include health information), and an inferred mood score.

HR says no one is dismissed on the basis of the tool, and a team lead always reviews the data first.
